# AI Usage and Impact on Students and Professionals

## Project Overview

This project analyzes how students and professionals use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, the tasks they use them for, and the benefits they report in terms of productivity, accuracy, satisfaction, and time saved.

The analysis was based on survey data from 288 respondents across 12 countries. The aim was to identify usage patterns, compare outcomes, and provide recommendations on how individuals and organizations can get more value from AI tools.

## Project Objectives

- Understand how students and professionals use AI tools.
- Identify the most commonly used AI tools and their purposes.
- Analyze the relationship between AI usage hours, productivity, and time saved.
- Compare reported outcomes across user groups, tools, and countries.
- Identify challenges affecting user satisfaction and trust.
- Provide data-driven recommendations based on the findings.

## Dataset Overview

- **Total respondents:** 288
- **Students:** 151
- **Professionals:** 137
- **Countries represented:** 12
- **Data fields:** 19
- **Analysis areas:** Demographics, AI usage, productivity, accuracy, satisfaction, cost, and time saved.

The dataset included information about respondents' backgrounds, AI tools used, purposes of use, daily usage hours, productivity scores, accuracy ratings, satisfaction scores, and estimated weekly time savings.

## Data Cleaning and Preparation

Before drawing conclusions, the raw dataset was reviewed and cleaned to improve consistency.

Key steps included:

- Standardizing AI tool names, such as combining variations of ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot.
- Correcting inconsistent country name capitalization.
- Calculating averages using records with available values.
- Reviewing existing pivot tables and charts against recalculated results.
- Examining relationships between usage hours, productivity, satisfaction, and time saved using correlation coefficients.
- Identifying missing values and unusual entries that could affect the results.

## Key Findings

### 1. AI Usage Is Widespread

Respondents reported an average of **2.9 hours of AI use per day**, with a median of 2 hours.

ChatGPT was the most commonly used tool, with 119 respondents (41%), followed by Gemini, Copilot, and Claude.

### 2. AI Usage Is Associated with Higher Reported Productivity

The analysis found a strong positive correlation between daily AI usage and weekly time saved (**0.78**). Usage hours also had a positive correlation with productivity (**0.66**).

Respondents using AI for more than 6 hours daily reported an average productivity score of 7.8 out of 10 and saved an estimated 14 hours per week.

These relationships do not prove that increased usage directly causes better results.

### 3. Reported Benefits Vary by Task

Writing and office work showed the highest average weekly time savings, at approximately 7 hours.

Research users reported the highest average daily AI usage and the highest satisfaction among the analyzed task
Coding was the most common use case but had the lowest average productivity score among the named purposes.

4. Accuracy Remains a Concern

The average AI accuracy rating was 3.4 out of 5, while about half of respondents rated accuracy at 3 or below.

Accuracy was positively associated with satisfaction, highlighting the importance of checking AI-generated information before relying on it.

5. Paid Tools Did Not Show Better Outcomes

Free users reported a slightly higher average productivity score than paying users (5.4 compared with 5.1).

The analysis found little relationship between AI spending and reported productivity, time saved, or satisfaction. This suggests that paying for a subscription does not automatically lead to better outcomes.

6. Usage Does Not Always Translate into Productivity

Respondents from Nigeria reported relatively high AI usage, averaging 4 hours daily, but a below-average productivity score of 4.8 out of 10.

This finding suggests an area for further investigation. However, the sample and available data are not sufficient to establish why this pattern occurred.

Recommendations

Based on the findings, the report recommends:

Improve AI training: Help occasional users develop practical and consistent AI usage habits.

Verify AI outputs: Encourage fact-checking, source validation, and code testing before using generated results.

Focus on high-value tasks: Prioritize writing, office work, and research workflows where respondents reported greater time savings.

Support coding users: Provide targeted training in prompting, testing, and reviewing AI-generated code.

Review subscription costs: Evaluate paid AI tools against specific business needs before approving subscriptions.

Investigate differences between user groups: Conduct follow-up interviews or surveys to understand why some heavy users report lower productivity.

Improve data collection: Standardize response options, validate numeric entries, make important fields mandatory, and clearly state currency.

Track performance over time: Use a dashboard to monitor usage hours, time saved, productivity, accuracy, and recommendation rates.

Limitations
The findings should be interpreted with care because:
The survey responses were self-reported rather than independently measured.
Correlation does not establish causation.
Some tools and countries had small respondent samples.
Missing values and unusual entries affected parts of the dataset.
The survey represents a snapshot and does not show how AI usage outcomes change over time.
The results are therefore indicative of the surveyed respondents and should not automatically be generalized to all AI users.

Conclusion

The analysis suggests that AI can provide useful benefits to students and professionals, particularly in terms of time savings and reported productivity. However, the benefits vary by task and level of usage, while concerns about accuracy remain important.

The findings point to practical opportunities in AI training, output verification, workflow selection, and data quality improvement. Rather than assuming that more expensive tools deliver better results, individuals and organizations should focus on how effectively AI is applied to specific tasks.
